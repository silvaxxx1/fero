## Design Context

### Users
**Audience**: One person — Fero, the creator's romantic partner. This is a private gift, not a public product. She opens it on or around her birthday (August 31) and likely views it alone or with the creator present. She is Arabic-speaking (Egyptian dialect based on love letter content), probably on a mobile phone or laptop.

**Context of use**: A moment of intimacy and surprise. She clicks a link, doesn't know what to expect, and the site unfolds as a gift. The experience is consumed once deeply, not repeatedly browsed.

**Job to be done**: Make her feel seen, adored, and delighted — not impressed by technical capability. The site is a love letter in interface form.

### Brand Personality
**Three words**: Playful, Dreamy, Sophisticated

**Emotional goal**: The feeling you get when someone makes you laugh and takes your breath away at the same time. Like an inside joke elevated into something beautiful. Giddy but not silly.

**Voice**: Intimate and personal. Warm but not saccharine. Surprising but never overwhelming.

**Anti-references**:
- NOT: Generic pink party site (floating hearts, emoji soup, pink gradients — the current design)
- NOT: Corporate/sterile (too polished, too cold, too SaaS)
- NOT: Kitschy/tacky (too many effects, visual noise over emotion)

### Aesthetic Direction
**Visual tone**: Dreamy Editorial — think Vogue Arabia meets a handmade birthday card. The sophistication of a magazine spread with the warmth of something made by hand, just for you.

**Theme**: Light mode. Warm cream base (not pure white), soft peach and dusty rose accents, with one deep contrasting color for editorial weight.

**Color palette direction**:
- Base: Warm cream, not clinical white — something like oklch(96% 0.015 50) — gently peachy
- Secondary surface: A soft dusty rose for accents and containers
- Accent: One deep, saturated color for punctuation — a terracotta or deep plum
- Text: Near-black with warmth, never pure black
- Avoid: cyan, teal, neon pink, the current pink-to-teal gradient palette

**Typography direction**:
- Display font: Something that reads as editorial and has personality — warm, a little retro-magazine, not a standard serif or sans-serif. Gloock, Young Serif, or a similarly distinctive editorial face.
- Body font: Clean and modern to contrast the display — Nunito is too cute; something like Jost, Geologica, or Raleway (not banned). Pair tight.
- Arabic: Amiri remains the right choice — it's genuinely beautiful and dignified for love letters.
- DO NOT use: Any font from the reflex banned list. System fonts are unacceptable.

**Layout principles**:
- Magazine-style asymmetry — not everything centered, sections that breathe differently from each other
- Big typographic moments punctuating the scroll — a single Arabic phrase at 5rem is more powerful than 20 floating hearts
- The gallery should feel like a lookbook or photo essay, not a stock photo grid

### Design Principles

1. **Playful sophistication**: Every animated or decorative element must earn its place through meaning, not just motion. A single well-timed heart is better than 200 confetti pieces.

2. **Editorial breathing room**: Generous whitespace between sections lets emotional moments land. Not every section competes for attention — some exist to create contrast for the ones that matter.

3. **Arabic as a design feature**: The Arabic love letters are the emotional core of this site. They should be set beautifully — large, dignified, centered in real white space — not crammed into a card alongside English.

4. **Surprise over saturation**: One genuinely unexpected moment (an unusual layout, an unexpected type choice, a subtle parallax that reveals something) is more memorable than ten sparkle animations.

5. **Intimacy at scale**: The site should feel like it was made for one person, even though it's a full webpage. Avoid anything that reads as "template" or "generic birthday website." Everything should feel chosen, not default.
